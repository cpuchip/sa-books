The direction of any given contribution to the magnetic field at point P is determined by the cross product $d\vec{\ell}\times\vec{r}$. Now the cross product of two vectors, when not zero, is always perpendicular to each of the vectors in the cross product, thus it is perpendicular to the plane defined by the two vectors. In the case at hand, both $d\vec{\ell}$ and $\vec{r}$ lie in the x-y plane so every $d\vec{B}$ due to the current in the wire will be in the +z or -z direction.

A bunch of vectors that are all parallel to one and the same line add like scalars. Hence, the difficulties that arise in the case of the electric field do not arise in the specified case of the magnetic field.

## Question 3

## 0 of 20 points

Regarding cases involving the application of the Biot-Savart Law to calculate the magnetic field due to an electric current, which of the following statements about why it is relatively easy to calculate the magnetic field due to a circular current loop, at the center of that loop, is most correct?

Selected Answer: ✗

The $d\vec{\ell}$ and the $\vec{r}$ in the cross product $d\vec{\ell}\times\vec{r}$ are always at right angles to each other so the magnitude of the cross product is simply $r\,d\ell$, but, one still has to deal with the fact that the vector $\vec{r}$ has a different magnitude for different elements of the current loop.

Correct Answer: ✓

For the case in question, $\vec{r}$ has the same magnitude for every current element, and, $d\vec{\ell}\times\vec{r}$ reduces to $r\,d\ell$.
