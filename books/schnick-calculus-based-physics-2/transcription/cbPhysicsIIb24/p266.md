Solving this for $\frac{1}{-i_{1}}$ yields $\frac{1}{-i_{1}}=\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{o}_{1}}-\frac{1}{f_{1}}$ which, when substituted into $\frac{1}{f_{2}}=\frac{1}{-i_{1}}+\frac{1}{i_{2}}$ from above, yields:

$$
\frac{1}{f_{2}}=\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{o}_{1}}-\frac{1}{f_{1}}+\frac{1}{i_{2}}
$$

which can be written as:

$$
\frac{1}{f_{1}}+\frac{1}{f_{2}}=\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{o}_{1}}+\frac{1}{i_{2}}
$$

The object distance $\boldsymbol{o}_{1}$ for the first lens is the object distance for the pair of lenses. I'm going to call that $\boldsymbol{o}_{\mathrm{c}}$ for the object distance for the combination of two lenses. In other words, $\boldsymbol{o}_{\mathrm{c}} \equiv \boldsymbol{o}_{1}$. The image distance for the second lens is the image distance for the pair two lenses. I'm going to call that $i_{\mathrm{c}}$ for the image distance for the combination of two lenses. In other words, $i_{\mathrm{c}} \equiv i_{2}$. Thus, on the right, we have the reciprocal of the object distance for the two-lens system plus the reciprocal of the image distance for the two-lens system.

$$
\frac{1}{f_{1}}+\frac{1}{f_{2}}=\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{o}_{\mathrm{c}}}+\frac{1}{i_{\mathrm{c}}}
$$

On the left we have the sum of the powers of the two lenses.

$$
P_{1}+P_{2}=\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{o}_{\mathrm{c}}}+\frac{1}{i_{\mathrm{c}}}
$$

For a single lens, the lens equation $\frac{1}{f}=\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{o}}+\frac{1}{i}$ expressed in terms of the power, reads $P=\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{o}}+\frac{1}{i}$. Thus, if, for our combination of two lenses at one and the same location, we identify $P_{1}+P_{2}$ as the power $P_{\mathrm{c}}$ of the combination of two lenses, we have,

$$
P_{\mathrm{c}}=\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{o}_{\mathrm{c}}}+\frac{1}{i_{\mathrm{c}}}
$$

Thus, a pair of lenses, each of which is at essentially one and the same location, acts as a single lens whose power $P_{\mathrm{c}}$ is the sum of the powers of the two lenses making up the combination.

$$
P_{\mathrm{c}}=P_{1}+P_{2} \tag{29-4}
$$
