Recalling that a tesla is a $\frac{\text{N} \cdot \text{m}}{\text{A} \cdot \text{m}^{2}}$ we have:

$$\tau = 8.8 \times 10^{-7}\ \text{A} \cdot \text{m}^{2} \cdot \frac{\text{N} \cdot \text{m}}{\text{A} \cdot \text{m}^{2}}$$

$$\tau = 8.8 \times 10^{-7}\ \text{N} \cdot \text{m}$$

Thus, the torque on the magnetic dipole is $8.8 \times 10^{-7}$ N·m clockwise, as viewed from the vantage point of the creator of the diagram.

## Example 15-2

A particle having a magnetic dipole moment $\vec{\boldsymbol{\mu}} = 0.025\ \text{A} \cdot \text{m}^2\ \hat{\imath} - 0.035\ \text{A} \cdot \text{m}^2\ \hat{\jmath} + 0.015\ \text{A} \cdot \text{m}^2\ \hat{k}$ is at a point in space where the magnetic field $\vec{\mathbf{B}} = 2.3\ \text{mT}\ \hat{\imath} + 5.3\ \text{mT}\ \hat{\jmath} - 3.6\ \text{mT}\ \hat{k}$.
Find the torque exerted on the particle by the magnetic field.

$$\vec{\boldsymbol{\tau}} = \vec{\boldsymbol{\mu}} \times \vec{\mathbf{B}}$$

$$\vec{\boldsymbol{\tau}} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{\imath} & \hat{\jmath} & \hat{k} \\ 0.025\ \text{Am}^2 & -0.035\ \text{Am}^2 & 0.015\ \text{Am}^2 \\ 0.0023 \frac{\text{Nm}}{\text{Am}^2} & 0.0053 \frac{\text{Nm}}{\text{Am}^2} & -0.0036 \frac{\text{Nm}}{\text{Am}^2} \end{vmatrix}$$

$$\vec{\boldsymbol{\tau}} = \hat{\imath} \left[ \left(-0.035\ \text{Am}^2\right) \left(-0.0036 \frac{\text{Nm}}{\text{Am}^2}\right) - \left(0.015\ \text{Am}^2\right) \left(0.0053 \frac{\text{Nm}}{\text{Am}^2}\right) \right]$$

$$+ \hat{\jmath} \left[ \left(0.015\ \text{Am}^2\right) \left(0.0023 \frac{\text{Nm}}{\text{Am}^2}\right) - \left(0.025\ \text{Am}^2\right) \left(-0.0036 \frac{\text{Nm}}{\text{Am}^2}\right) \right]$$

$$+ \hat{k} \left[ \left(0.025\ \text{Am}^2\right) \left(0.0053 \frac{\text{Nm}}{\text{Am}^2}\right) - \left(-0.035\ \text{Am}^2\right) \left(0.0023 \frac{\text{Nm}}{\text{Am}^2}\right) \right]$$

$$\vec{\boldsymbol{\tau}} = 1.2 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{Nm}\ \hat{\imath} - 1.2 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{Nm}\ \hat{\jmath} + 2.1 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{Nm}\ \hat{k}$$
