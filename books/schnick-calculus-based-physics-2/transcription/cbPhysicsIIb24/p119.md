And the gradient of $\vec{\mu} \cdot \vec{\mathbf{B}}$ (which by equation 15-2 is the force we seek) is given by

$$\nabla (\vec{\mu} \cdot \vec{\mathbf{B}}) = \frac{\partial (\vec{\mu} \cdot \vec{\mathbf{B}})}{\partial x} \hat{\imath} + \frac{\partial (\vec{\mu} \cdot \vec{\mathbf{B}})}{\partial y} \hat{\jmath} + \frac{\partial (\vec{\mu} \cdot \vec{\mathbf{B}})}{\partial z} \hat{k}$$

where derivatives in this equation can (using $\vec{\mu} \cdot \vec{\mathbf{B}} = \mu_x B_x + \mu_y B_y + \mu_z B_z$ from just above) can be expressed as:

$$\frac{\partial (\vec{\mu} \cdot \vec{\mathbf{B}})}{\partial x} = \mu_x \frac{\partial B_x}{\partial x} + \mu_y \frac{\partial B_y}{\partial x} + \mu_z \frac{\partial B_z}{\partial x},$$

$$\frac{\partial (\vec{\mu} \cdot \vec{\mathbf{B}})}{\partial y} = \mu_x \frac{\partial B_x}{\partial y} + \mu_y \frac{\partial B_y}{\partial y} + \mu_z \frac{\partial B_z}{\partial y},\ \text{and}$$

$$\frac{\partial (\vec{\mu} \cdot \vec{\mathbf{B}})}{\partial z} = \mu_x \frac{\partial B_x}{\partial z} + \mu_y \frac{\partial B_y}{\partial z} + \mu_z \frac{\partial B_z}{\partial z};$$

where we have taken advantage of the fact that the components of the magnetic dipole moment of the victim are not functions of position. Also note that the derivatives are all partial derivatives. Partial derivatives are the easy kind in the sense that, when, for instance, you take the derivative with respect to $x$, you are to treat $y$ and $z$ as if they were constants. Finally, it is important to realize that, after you take the derivatives, you have to plug the values of $x, y,$ and $z$ corresponding to the location of the magnetic dipole (the victim), into the given expression for the force.

## Example 15-3

There exists, in a region of space, a magnetic field, given in terms of Cartesian unit vectors by:

$$\vec{\mathbf{B}} = -5.82 \times 10^{-6} \text{T} \cdot \text{m} \frac{\text{y}}{x^2 + y^2} \hat{\imath} + 5.82 \times 10^{-6} \text{T} \cdot \text{m} \frac{\text{x}}{x^2 + y^2} \hat{\jmath}$$

A particle is in the region of space where the magnetic field exists. The particle has a magnetic dipole moment given by:

$$\vec{\mu} = .514 \text{ A} \cdot \text{m}^2\ \hat{\imath}$$

The particle is at $(0.110 \text{ m}, 0, 0)$.

Find the force exerted on the particle by the magnetic field.
