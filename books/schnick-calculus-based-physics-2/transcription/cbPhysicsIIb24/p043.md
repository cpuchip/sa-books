$$\mathcal{W} = kq'q \frac{x^{-1}}{-1} \bigg|_{x_1}^{x_2}$$

$$\mathcal{W} = -kq'q \left( \frac{1}{x_2} - \frac{1}{x_1} \right)$$

$$\mathcal{W} = -\left( \frac{kq'q}{x_2} - \frac{kq'q}{x_1} \right)$$

Compare this with the following solution to the same problem (a particle of charge $q'$ is fixed at the origin and we need to find the work done by the electric field of that particle on a victim of charge $q$ as the victim moves along the x axis from $x_1$ to $x_2$):

$$\mathcal{W} = -\Delta U$$

$$\mathcal{W} = -q \Delta \varphi$$

$$\mathcal{W} = -q \left( \varphi_2 - \varphi_1 \right)$$

$$\mathcal{W} = -q \left( \frac{kq'}{x_2} - \frac{kq'}{x_1} \right)$$

$$\mathcal{W} = -\left( \frac{kq'q}{x_2} - \frac{kq'q}{x_1} \right)$$
