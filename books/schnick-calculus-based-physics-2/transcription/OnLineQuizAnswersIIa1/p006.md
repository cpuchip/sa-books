$$F = \frac{kq_1q_2}{r^2}$$

$$F' = \frac{kq_1q_2}{r'^2}$$

SUBSTITUTE $r' = \frac{1}{2}r$

$$F' = \frac{kq_1q_2}{(\frac{1}{2}r)^2}$$

$$F' = \frac{kq_1q_2}{\frac{1}{4}r^2}$$

$$F' = 4\underbrace{\frac{kq_1q_2}{r^2}}_{\text{THIS IS F (SEE FIRST LINE)}}$$

$$F' = 4F$$

$$F' = 4(.12\ \text{N})$$

$$\boxed{F' = .48\ \text{N}}$$

Here's another way:

① $F = \frac{kq_1q_2}{r^2}$

② $F' = \frac{kq_1q_2}{r'^2}$

Form the ratio $\frac{F'}{F}$:

$$\frac{F'}{F} = \frac{\left(\frac{kq_1q_2}{r'^2}\right)}{\left(\frac{kq_1q_2}{r^2}\right)}$$

Dividing by a fraction
is the same as
multiplying by the reciprocal:

$$\frac{F'}{F} = \frac{\cancel{kq_1q_2}}{r'^2} \cdot \frac{r^2}{\cancel{kq_1q_2}}$$
