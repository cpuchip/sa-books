$$F = \frac{kq_1q_2}{r^2}$$

$$F' = \frac{kq_1q_2}{r'^2}$$

substitute $r' = 2r$

$$F' = \frac{kq_1q_2}{(2r)^2}$$

$$F' = \frac{kq_1q_2}{4r^2}$$

$$F' = \frac{1}{4} \underbrace{\frac{kq_1q_2}{r^2}}_{\substack{\text{This is } F \\ \text{(see 1st line)}}}$$

$$F' = \frac{1}{4} F$$

$$F' = \frac{1}{4} (.12\ \text{N})$$

$$\boxed{F' = .03\ \text{N}}$$

Here's another way:

① $F = \frac{kq_1q_2}{r^2}$

② $F' = \frac{kq_1q_2}{r'^2}$

Form the ratio $\frac{F'}{F}$:

$$\frac{F'}{F} = \frac{\left(\frac{kq_1q_2}{r'^2}\right)}{\left(\frac{kq_1q_2}{r^2}\right)}$$
