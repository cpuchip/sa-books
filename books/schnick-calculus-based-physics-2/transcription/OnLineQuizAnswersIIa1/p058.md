Consider a capacitor which is charged to 12 volts and then connected across a resistor. Let $t_{1/2}$ be the time it takes for the voltage to drop down to 6 volts. Suppose the experiment is repeated, but with a resistor whose resistance is twice that of the original resistor. How does the new $t_{1/2}$ compare with the original $t_{1/2}$?

Selected Answer: ✗ The new $t_{1/2}$ is the same as the original $t_{1/2}$.

Correct Answer: ✓ The new $t_{1/2}$ is twice the original $t_{1/2}$.

Feedback:

$$V = V_0 e^{-t/\tau}$$

At $t = t_{1/2}$ $V = \frac{1}{2} V_0$

So:

$$\frac{1}{2} V_0 = V_0 e^{-t_{1/2}/\tau}$$

$$\frac{1}{2} = e^{-t_{1/2}/\tau}$$

$$\ln \frac{1}{2} = -\frac{t_{1/2}}{\tau}$$

$$\frac{t_{1/2}}{\tau} = -\ln \frac{1}{2}$$

$$\frac{t_{1/2}}{\tau} = \ln 2$$

$$t_{1/2} = (\ln 2) \tau$$

but $\tau = RC$

$$t_{1/2} = (\ln 2) RC$$

Doubling $R$ in this expression results in a doubling of $t_{1/2}$.
