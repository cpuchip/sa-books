But, except for the replacement of $-y$ by $x$, this is the same expression that we had for $dB_x$. And those, (the $-y$ in the expression for $dB_x$ and the $x$ in the expression for $dB_y$), are, as far as the integration over $z'$ goes, constants, out front. They don't affect the integration, they just “go along for the ride.” So, we can use our $B_x$ result for $B_y$ if we just replace the $-y$, in our expression for $B_x$, with $x$. In other words, without having to go through the entire integration process again, we have:

$$B_y = \frac{\mu_o I}{2\pi} x \frac{1}{x^2 + y^2}$$

Since we have no z component in our expression

$$\mathbf{d}\vec{\mathbf{B}} = -\frac{\mu_o I}{4\pi} y \frac{dz'}{[x^2 + y^2 + (z - z')^2]^{3/2}} \hat{\imath} + \frac{\mu_o I}{4\pi} x \frac{dz'}{[x^2 + y^2 + (z - z')^2]^{3/2}} \hat{\jmath},$$

$\vec{\mathbf{B}}$ itself must have no z component.

Substituting our results for $B_x$, $B_y$, and $B_z$ into the $\hat{\imath}$, $\hat{\jmath}$, $\hat{k}$ expression for $\vec{\mathbf{B}}$, (Namely, $\vec{\mathbf{B}} = B_x \hat{\imath} + B_y \hat{\jmath} + B_z \hat{k}$ ), we have:

$$\vec{\mathbf{B}} = -\frac{\mu_o I}{2\pi} y \frac{1}{x^2 + y^2} \hat{\imath} + \frac{\mu_o I}{2\pi} x \frac{1}{x^2 + y^2} \hat{\jmath} + 0 \hat{k}$$

$$\vec{\mathbf{B}} = \frac{\mu_o I}{2\pi} \frac{1}{x^2 + y^2} (-y \hat{\imath} + x \hat{\jmath})$$

The quantity $x^2 + y^2$ is just $r^2$, the square of the distance that point P is from the current-carrying wire (recall that we are finding the magnetic field due to a wire, with a current $I$, that extends along the z axis from $-\infty$ to $+\infty$)

$$\vec{\mathbf{B}} = \frac{\mu_o I}{2\pi} \frac{1}{r^2} (-y \hat{\imath} + x \hat{\jmath})$$

Furthermore, the vector $(-y \hat{\imath} + x \hat{\jmath})$ has magnitude $\sqrt{(-y)^2 + x^2} = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} = r$. Hence, the unit vector $\hat{\mathbf{u}}_B$ in the same direction as $(-y \hat{\imath} + x \hat{\jmath})$ is given by

$$\hat{\mathbf{u}}_B = \frac{-y \hat{\imath} + x \hat{\jmath}}{r} = -\frac{y}{r} \hat{\imath} + \frac{x}{r} \hat{\jmath}$$
