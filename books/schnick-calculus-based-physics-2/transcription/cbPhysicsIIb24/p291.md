$$\frac{\partial \varphi}{\partial y} = \frac{kq \left( y + \frac{d}{2} \right)}{\left[ x^2 + \left( y + \frac{d}{2} \right)^2 \right]^{\frac{3}{2}}} - \frac{kq \left( y - \frac{d}{2} \right)}{\left[ x^2 + \left( y - \frac{d}{2} \right)^2 \right]^{\frac{3}{2}}}$$

Again, we were asked to find the electric field on the x axis, so, we evaluate this expression at $y = 0$:

$$\left. \frac{\partial \varphi}{\partial y} \right|_{y=0} = \frac{kq \left( 0 + \frac{d}{2} \right)}{\left[ x^2 + \left( 0 + \frac{d}{2} \right)^2 \right]^{\frac{3}{2}}} - \frac{kq \left( 0 - \frac{d}{2} \right)}{\left[ x^2 + \left( 0 - \frac{d}{2} \right)^2 \right]^{\frac{3}{2}}}$$

$$\left. \frac{\partial \varphi}{\partial y} \right|_{y=0} = \frac{kqd}{\left[ x^2 + \frac{d^2}{4} \right]^{\frac{3}{2}}}$$

Plugging $\left.\frac{\partial \varphi}{\partial x}\right|_{y=0} = 0$ and $\left.\frac{\partial \varphi}{\partial y}\right|_{y=0} = \frac{kqd}{\left[ x^2 + \frac{d^2}{4} \right]^{\frac{3}{2}}}$ into $\vec{\mathbf{E}} = -\left( \frac{\partial \varphi}{\partial x} \hat{\imath} + \frac{\partial \varphi}{\partial y} \hat{\jmath} \right)$ yields:

$$\vec{\mathbf{E}} = -\left( 0 \hat{\imath} + \frac{kqd}{\left[ x^2 + \frac{d^2}{4} \right]^{\frac{3}{2}}} \hat{\jmath} \right)$$

$$\vec{\mathbf{E}} = -\frac{kqd}{\left[ x^2 + \frac{d^2}{4} \right]^{\frac{3}{2}}} \hat{\jmath}$$

As expected, $\vec{E}$ is in the $-y$ direction. Note that to find the electric field on the x axis, you have to take the derivatives first, and then evaluate at $y = 0$.
