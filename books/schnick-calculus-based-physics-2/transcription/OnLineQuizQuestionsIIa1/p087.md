## Question 5

## 20 points

The x-component of the electric field characterized by an electric potential  $V(x,y,z)$  can be expressed as -dV/dx holding y and z constant. This would suggest that the units of E would be the units of V divided by the units of x, that is, in SI units, volts per meter. Are the units of E volts per meter? If not, what is wrong with the argument above?

○ Yes.

No. One cannot treat dV as if it had the same units of V and one cannot treat dx as if it had the same units of x.

No. The units of E are N/C (newtons per coulomb).
