$$V = \frac{kQ}{L} \ln \left[ \frac{L - x + \sqrt{(L - x)^2 + y^2}}{-x + \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}} \right]$$

$$V = \frac{kQ}{L} \ln \left\{ L - x + \left[ (L - x)^2 + y^2 \right]^{1/2} \right\} - \frac{kQ}{L} \ln \left[ \left( x^2 + y^2 \right)^{1/2} - x^2 \right]$$

$$E_y = -\frac{\partial V}{\partial y}$$

$$E_y = -\frac{kQ}{L} \left\{ L - x + \left[ (L - x)^2 + y^2 \right]^{1/2} \right\}^{-1} \frac{1}{2} \left( (L - x)^2 + y^2 \right)^{-1/2} 2y$$

$$- - \frac{kQ}{L} \left[ \left( x^2 + y^2 \right)^{1/2} - x^2 \right]^{-1} \frac{1}{2} \left( x^2 + y^2 \right)^{-1/2} 2y$$

$$E_y = -\frac{kQy}{L} \left\{ L - x + \left[ (L - x)^2 + y^2 \right]^{1/2} \right\}^{-1} \left( (L - x)^2 + y^2 \right)^{-1/2}$$

$$+ \frac{kQy}{L} \left[ \left( x^2 + y^2 \right)^{1/2} - x^2 \right]^{-1} \left( x^2 + y^2 \right)^{-1/2}$$

This is the expression for the y-component of electric field valid at all points in space. We are asked for the y-component of the electric field on the y-axis, that is to say, at $x=0$. Before simplifying the above expression any further, we go ahead and substitute $x=0$.

$$E_y(0, y) = -\frac{kQy}{L} \left\{ L + \left[ L^2 + y^2 \right]^{1/2} \right\}^{-1} \left( L^2 + y^2 \right)^{-1/2} + \frac{kQy}{L} \frac{1}{y} \frac{1}{y}$$

$$E_y(0, y) = \frac{kQ}{Ly} \left\{ 1 - \frac{y^2}{\left( L + \sqrt{L^2 + y^2} \right) \sqrt{L^2 + y^2}} \right\}$$

$$E_y(0, y) = \frac{kQ}{Ly} \left\{ 1 - \frac{y^2}{L^2 + y^2 + L \sqrt{L^2 + y^2}} \right\}$$
