## The Dipole-Dipole Force

The magnetic field produced by one bar magnet will exert a torque on another bar magnet. Because the magnetic field due to a magnetic dipole is non-uniform (you can see in $\vec{\mathbf{B}} = \frac{\mu_{\mathrm{o}}}{4\pi}\frac{3(\vec{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{r}})\hat{\mathbf{r}} - \vec{\boldsymbol{\mu}}}{r^{3}}$ that it dies off like $\frac{1}{r^{3}}$), it also exerts a *force* on another bar magnet.

We are now in a position to say something quantitative about the force that one bar magnet exerts on another. Consider an object that is at the origin of a Cartesian coordinate system. Suppose that object to have a magnetic dipole moment given by $\vec{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_1 = \mu_1\hat{\imath}$. Clearly we're talking about a magnet pointing (treating the magnet as an arrow with its head at the north pole of the magnet) in the +x direction. Let's find the force that *that* magnet would exert on another one at $(x, 0, 0)$ given that the magnetic dipole moment of the second magnet is $\vec{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_2 = -\mu_2\hat{\imath}$. The second magnet is pointing back toward the origin, so we are talking about two magnets whose north poles are facing each other. Knowing that like poles repel, you should be able to anticipate that the second magnet will experience a force in the +x direction. The magnetic field produced by the first magnet is given (for any point in space, as long as the distance to that point, from the origin, is large compared to the size of the magnet) by

$$\vec{\mathbf{B}} = \frac{\mu_{\mathrm{o}}}{4\pi}\frac{3(\vec{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{r}})\hat{\mathbf{r}} - \vec{\boldsymbol{\mu}}}{r^{3}}$$

$$\vec{\mathbf{B}}_1 = \frac{\mu_{\mathrm{o}}}{4\pi}\frac{3(\mu_1\hat{\imath} \cdot\hat{\mathbf{r}})\hat{\mathbf{r}} - \mu_1\hat{\imath}}{r^{3}}$$

$$\vec{\mathbf{B}}_1 = \frac{\mu_{\mathrm{o}}}{4\pi}\left(\frac{3(\mu_1\hat{\imath} \cdot\vec{\mathbf{r}})\vec{\mathbf{r}}}{r^{5}} - \frac{\mu_1\hat{\imath}}{r^{3}}\right)$$

The force on the second particle is given by:

$$\vec{\mathbf{F}} = \nabla(\vec{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_2 \cdot \vec{\mathbf{B}}_1)$$

evaluated at the position of magnet 2, namely at $(x, 0, 0)$. Substituting the given $\vec{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_2 = -\mu_2\hat{\imath}$ in for the magnetic dipole of particle 2, and, the expression just above for $\vec{\mathbf{B}}_1$, we obtain:

$$\vec{\mathbf{F}} = \nabla\left\{-\mu_2\hat{\imath} \cdot\left[\frac{\mu_{\mathrm{o}}}{4\pi}\left(\frac{3(\mu_1\hat{\imath} \cdot\vec{\mathbf{r}})\vec{\mathbf{r}}}{r^{5}} - \frac{\mu_1\hat{\imath}}{r^{3}}\right)\right]\right\}$$

$$\vec{\mathbf{F}} = -\frac{\mu_{\mathrm{o}}}{4\pi}\mu_1\mu_2\nabla\left\{\hat{\imath} \cdot\left[\left(\frac{3(\hat{\imath} \cdot\vec{\mathbf{r}})\vec{\mathbf{r}}}{r^{5}} - \frac{\hat{\imath}}{r^{3}}\right)\right]\right\}$$
